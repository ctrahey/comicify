## What makes a good product comic

A product comic is a short sequential-art story that helps a product team see a
product idea in its human context: who uses it, in what situation, what they
feel, and what the product does at the moment that matters. It replaces pages of
requirements with something a whole team can grasp in a minute and argue about
productively. (Background: Kevin Cheng, *See What I Mean*; Scott McCloud,
*Understanding Comics*.)

Principles:

1. **One key message.** Every panel serves a single product insight. If a beat
   doesn't advance it, cut it.
2. **Show time, function, and emotion together.** Comics are good at showing a
   sequence of events, how a product behaves at each step, and how the person
   feels about it. A good product comic uses all three.
3. **Abstraction is a feature.** Simple, iconic characters (even animals) let
   readers project themselves into the story and avoid arguments about
   demographics. Backgrounds can carry more detail to make the setting clear.
4. **Interfaces stay vague.** Show only the UI needed to understand the moment,
   drawn in the comic's own style. Never a realistic screenshot: realism pulls
   attention to visual design instead of the experience.
5. **Make the product's logic legible.** When a feature depends on a rule
   (a threshold, a trigger, a condition), give the reader a panel or a screen
   that lets them *see* the rule at work, not just its outcome.
6. **Motivation before mechanism.** Readers must understand why a character
   reaches for the product before they care how it works.
7. **Failure is a valid subject.** Comics can be cautionary: showing how a
   product fails (or how an unrelated product decision undermines a good
   feature) is often the most valuable story a team can see.
8. **Short.** Most product comics are one to three pages. Each panel earns its
   place; a 6–12 panel comic is typical.
9. **Readable at presentation size.** Comics are usually shown as slides on a
   call. Keep dialogue short, text large, and each panel focused on one idea.
