## How adults learn from comics (apply these)

Product comics teach. Readers are busy adults; every panel competes for
attention. From multimedia-learning research (Mayer) and comics craft:

- **Show, don't tell.** If a line of dialogue refers to a thing ("like this
  one!"), the reader must *see* that thing — give it an insert panel or put it
  in frame. Artifacts (an invoice, a screen, a message) are the evidence.
- **One idea per panel.** A panel shows one moment and makes one point.
- **Coherence.** Cut what doesn't serve the act's objective: pleasantries,
  repeated beats, explanation the pictures already give.
- **Signaling.** Make the key information visually prominent: the number that
  matters, the button that changes state, the moment of decision.
- **Redundancy.** Don't say in words what the picture already shows; use words
  for what pictures can't carry (motive, rule, consequence).
- **Contiguity.** Words sit next to what they describe, in the same panel.
- **Segmenting.** Clear chunks: acts and scenes with visible boundaries,
  each with an establishing view of where we are.
- **Pre-training.** Show a named thing (a system, an app, a term) before or
  as it's first named; never rely on an unexplained name.
- **Dialogue economy.** Speech is short and spoken, not written prose; split
  long lines; prefer one strong line to three adequate ones.
